# Supervisory Analytics Tool for SOC Assessment (SAT-SA)

> A deployable analytics platform designed to assist NCIIPC supervisors in analysing SOC alert and case-management data at scale across Critical Sector Entities (CSEs).

## Background

The National Critical Information Infrastructure Protection Centre (NCIIPC) assesses the cyber resilience of Critical Sector Entities (CSEs). As part of these assessments, NCIIPC performs manual reviews of samples of security alerts and case-management records generated by Security Operations Centres (SOCs). These reviews consistently produce valuable supervisory findings that are not evident through policies, audits, self-assessments, management reports, KPI dashboards, or compliance documentation.

## Purpose

The purpose of these reviews is to use alert and case-management data as operational evidence to assess whether a CSE possesses effective capabilities relating to:

- (i) Threat Detection
- (ii) Investigation
- (iii) Escalation
- (iv) Incident Response
- (v) Security Operations
- (vi) Governance and Oversight
- (vii) Operational Discipline
- (viii) Cyber Resilience

## Problem Statement

Manual review is resource-intensive and difficult to scale across a growing number of CSEs and increasing volumes of security data. NCIIPC seeks a deployable **Supervisory Analytics Tool for SOC Assessment (SAT-SA)** that assists supervisors in analysing SOC alert and case-management data at scale. The tool should help supervisors:

1. Identify entities requiring supervisory attention.
2. Prioritise alert samples and investigations for manual review.
3. Detect operational weaknesses and cyber resilience concerns.
4. Improve the efficiency, consistency and scalability of supervisory assessments.

> **Note:** The tool is intended to support human examiners and supervisory decision-making. It is **not** intended to replace supervisory judgement.

## Scope

### In Scope
- Analysis of SOC alert and case-management data
- Identification of execution gaps and negative space
- Supervisory dashboards and reports
- Peer comparison and benchmarking
- Explainability and auditability of results

### Out of Scope
The proposed solution is **not** intended to:

1. Function or replace as a Security Operations Centre (SOC) of CSEs.
2. Perform real-time monitoring.
3. Act as a SIEM platform.
4. Act as a centralized SOC for multiple entities.
5. Continuously collect logs or telemetry from CSEs.
6. Serve as a national cyber monitoring platform.

The solution should be viewed as a **supervisory analytics capability**, not an operational security capability.

## Data Environment

Participants may assume access to periodic submissions including:

1. Alert metadata
2. Case management records
3. Investigation workflow data
4. Escalation records
5. Alert disposition and closure information
6. Asset and system inventory information (where available)

> Solutions should minimise dependence on raw logs, packet captures, customer information, or other sensitive operational data unless clearly justified.

## Core Supervisory Problems

Manual reviews often uncover weaknesses that are not visible through conventional reporting mechanisms. These weaknesses generally fall into two categories:

### A. Execution Gaps
Situations where documented controls, governance arrangements, policies, procedures, metrics or reported capabilities suggest effective operation, but operational evidence indicates otherwise.

**Examples:**
- Alerts acknowledged but not meaningfully investigated.
- Cases closed unusually quickly.
- Critical alerts closed without escalation.
- Repetitive or template-driven investigations.
- Controls deployed but not effectively monitored.
- Operational behaviour designed to satisfy metrics without reducing risk.

### B. Negative Space
Situations where expected evidence is absent.

**Examples:**
- Missing telemetry from critical systems.
- Absence of expected alert categories.
- Missing investigations or escalation records.
- Unexpectedly low activity levels.
- Monitoring blind spots.
- Absence of evidence that would normally be expected within comparable environments.

## Functional Requirements

### Data Ingestion
The tool shall:
1. Ingest structured data from multiple CSEs.
2. Support common formats such as CSV, JSON, database exports and APIs where available.
3. Support analysis of large datasets spanning multiple entities and time periods.

### Supervisory Analytics
The tool shall:
4. Identify indicators of detection, investigation and escalation weaknesses.
5. Detect potential execution gaps.
6. Detect potential negative space.
7. Identify anomalies, outliers and suspicious operational patterns.
8. Perform peer comparison and benchmarking across entities.
9. Generate entity-level supervisory risk indicators.
10. Prioritise entities, controls, processes and alert samples for manual review.

### Explainability
The tool shall:
11. Provide clear rationale for findings.
12. Present supporting evidence.
13. Support traceability and auditability of results.
14. Allow supervisors to understand why an entity or activity was flagged.

### Reporting
The tool shall:
15. Generate supervisory dashboards and reports.
16. Support trend analysis across entities and time periods.
17. Enable drill-down from supervisory findings to underlying evidence.

## Illustrative Supervisory Use Cases

The tool may assist in identifying:

1. High-severity alerts closed unusually quickly.
2. Repeated alerts on the same asset without evidence of root-cause remediation.
3. Critical alerts closed without appropriate escalation.
4. Critical systems generating little or no security telemetry.
5. Significant deviations from peer entities.
6. Missing monitoring coverage for critical environments.
7. Repetitive investigation patterns suggesting superficial review.
8. Operational behaviours that satisfy performance metrics without effectively managing cyber risk.
9. Investigation or escalation workloads inconsistent with expected activity levels.

## Deployment Requirements

The solution shall operate within an NCIIPC-controlled environment.

The solution must:
- Operate in a **fully offline (air-gapped) network**.
- Require **no Internet connectivity**.
- Have **no dependency on cloud services**.
- Have **no dependency on SaaS platforms**.
- Have **no dependency on externally hosted AI models or APIs**.
- Support **local deployment** and **local data processing**.

Where AI or machine learning is proposed, participants shall specify:
- Model architecture.
- Hardware requirements.
- Offline training and inference approach.
- Model update mechanism.
- Explainability controls.
- Auditability controls.

## Validation Requirements

Participants shall explain how the proposed solution will be validated against findings derived from expert manual review.

Solutions should demonstrate their ability to identify supervisory signals, operational weaknesses and areas requiring attention with effectiveness **comparable to or better than** current manual sampling approaches.

## Success Criterion

A successful solution will enable NCIIPC to efficiently analyse large volumes of SOC alert and case-management data from multiple CSEs, identify entities and operational areas requiring supervisory attention, prioritise manual review effort, and preserve the quality of supervisory assurance currently obtained through expert human examination.
